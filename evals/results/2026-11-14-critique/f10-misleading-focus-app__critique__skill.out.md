## Intent read

**Piece:** Landing-page hero and offer block for FocusForge, a focus-timer app — headline, proof line, social proof, mechanism, testimonial, scarcity, one CTA (11 lines, 74 words).
**Profile:** 3 · Website & landing page. Critical tiers: S1, S2, S3, T1, T2, L1.
**Reader:** Someone who struggles to concentrate and is browsing focus/productivity apps — problem-aware to solution-aware, in a crowded category where they have heard every focus promise already.
**Job:** Reader learns what FocusForge is, believes it will help, and signs up. Author gets a founding-member conversion that survives contact with the product.
**Constraints:** Health/wellness claims (regulated — FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance in the US, ASA/CAP in the UK, plus App Store and Play Store health-claim policies). No BRAND.md found; voice inferred from the piece. Supplied facts: internal survey of 140 users, 62% reported better focus after a week; 2M total downloads, ~60k monthly active users; no clinical trial; founding-member price has no end date.
**Confidence:** High on profile, job and claim status. Medium on whether this is the full page or the hero only — the findings hold either way.

## Reader's experience

A skeptical reader stops at "clinically proven to boost concentration by up to 247%" — the figure is too large and too precise to be believed, and an app that had run a trial would name it. A credulous reader reaches "[Claim my spot]" still not knowing what the app does or what it costs, with a midnight deadline pushing them to decide before they can find out. Both readers are lost: the first doesn't trust the page, the second can't evaluate the offer.

## Verdict: Rethink

Five P0 integrity failures, three of which are contradicted by the company's own facts — there is no clinical trial, the price has no end date, and the 2 million are downloads rather than transformed users. The page's entire differentiator is a claim the company says it does not hold.

**Highest-leverage next step:** Write down what FocusForge actually does and what it costs, then rebuild the page using only the four facts the company holds — 2M downloads, ~60k monthly actives, 62% of 140 surveyed reporting better focus in week one, 10-minute sessions. The honest version has enough material for a real page; it just isn't on this one yet.

## What works (keep these)

- **"in just 10 minutes a day"** — the only concrete, small, believable thing on the page. A reader can picture it and judge whether they can do it. This is the seed of an honest promise; every revision should keep it.
- **The sequence and the single ask.** Promise → proof → social proof → mechanism → testimonial → next step, with one CTA and no competing links. The skeleton is right. The problem is what each slot is filled with, not the order of the slots.
- **The prose discipline.** Short declarative sentences, no stock vocabulary, no "unlock" or "seamless," no puffery adjectives, no hedging — the linter found zero style tells in 74 words. The author does not need a different writer. They need true material to put in these sentences.

                  productivity") that the only evidence available — 62% of 140 reporting better focus — cannot
                  support for 140 people, let alone two million. Selective framing like this is the claim a

                  60,000 people choosing to open a focus app every month is proof of use, which is what the
                  reader is actually weighing.
  Intervention:   Use the active-user figure as the social proof. If the download count stays, label it as
                  downloads and drop the outcome attached to it.
  Example:        "60,000 people use FocusForge every month."

[P0 · Structure · Persuasive design] The scarcity and the deadline are not true
  Evidence:       "Only 37 founding-member spots left at this price. Offer ends at midnight." (line 9)

                  the midnight deadline inventions, and makes "Claim my spot" a CTA whose urgency is
                  manufactured. This is the pattern most likely to produce a chargeback, an app-store complaint

                  sees the same 37 spots and the same deadline, and now distrusts everything above it. Pressure
                  applied with information the reader would reject if they knew it crosses from persuasion
                  into manipulation.

                  quote itself is genuine.
