## Reader's experience

The reader is held by the first clause and does not get a chance to drift: a port of enormous machinery stops dead, and the reason turns out to be one man and a radio older than his apprentice. By the end of the third paragraph they have a character they trust, a writer who was visibly there, and one line — "nobody's watching" — that they will want explained. Nothing here asks to be skimmed, and nothing invites disbelief.

## Verdict: **Ship**

The opening does an opening's job in 139 words with no padding, no borrowed authority, and no line that any other writer would have produced. Highest-leverage next step is not in this text: "nobody's watching" is now the feature's engine, and the paragraph after this one has to say what it means without flattening it.

## What works (keep these)

- **The ninety-second frame as structure, not atmosphere.** "nothing moves at all" (para 1) → "Then he found the frequency, and the port started again" (para 3). The opening opens on stillness and closes on motion, and the same handset causes both. It is a complete figure, and the last line is load-bearing — do not let a later pass cut it as a soft landing.
- **Expertise shown instead of asserted.** "He knows which crane groans in a north wind and which one only pretends to." Nineteen years is a number; this clause is the evidence for it. The same move again in "arrive lit like apartment blocks and leave dark" — an image that carries information about the crew's night rather than decorating it.
- **The angle arrives in the subject's mouth.** "Nights, you're moving boxes and nobody's watching. It's a different job." The writer declines to say this themselves, which is why it is believable. Resist any revision that promotes it to the writer's voice or to the top.

## Notes

```
[P2 · Strategy · Credibility & claim integrity] One causal clause reaches past what either of you could see
  Evidence:       "He knows that the container ships arrive lit like apartment blocks and leave
                  dark, because by the time they leave, everyone aboard who can sleep is asleep."
                  (para 2)
  Why it matters: Every other fact here is either observed from the quay or framed as Okonjo's
                  knowledge. This one is about the berths of a departing ship. "He knows that X,
                  because Y" can be read two ways — Y as part of what Okonjo knows, or Y as the
                  writer's explanation — and most readers will take the second. A fact-checker will
                  query it, and it is the only sentence in the piece where the prose's authority
                  runs slightly ahead of the reporting.
  Intervention:   Attribution, not substance. If the reason is Okonjo's, say so; the anaphora of
                  "He knows" survives a sentence break.
  Example:        "...and leave dark. By the time they leave, he says, everyone aboard who can
                  sleep is asleep."
  Note:           If you already intend the whole proposition as his, nothing needs to change —
                  this is a comma-level choice, not an error.
```

```
[Alternative · Structure] "Nobody's watching" is doing two jobs, and the next beat inherits both
  Evidence:       "Nights, you're moving boxes and nobody's watching." (para 3)
  Why it matters: It can mean unsupervised (less oversight — shortcuts, safety, theft) or
                  unwitnessed (nobody sees this work at all). For an opening, holding both is a
                  door rather than a thesis, and that is a defensible choice. But the opening
                  spends its last line returning to motion, so the whole load of saying what the
                  feature is about falls on the paragraph after this one.
  Options:        (a) Resolve it in the nut graf and let the rest of the feature test it.
                  (b) Keep the ambiguity deliberately and let the structure answer it — in which
                  case the two meanings should be the piece's two halves, not a muddle.
  Note:           An option, not a correction. Nothing in the supplied text is wrong.
```
